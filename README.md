## Does Vrbo actually have a coupon code? No — and here's why the sites listing them are misleading

Vrbo has no guest-facing coupon system. Search for a rental, pick your dates, and head to checkout: there's no "enter promo code" box anywhere in the flow, because discounts on Vrbo are applied by hosts at the listing level and appear automatically in the price. Vrbo's own discount structure — weekly stays, monthly stays, new listings, last-minute dates — all price themselves in without any code.

That hasn't stopped coupon aggregators from publishing pages titled "Vrbo Coupon Code: 32% Off" or "9 Verified Vrbo Codes." Look closely at what those pages actually contain and the pattern is hard to miss. One popular page lists a "military discount, 10% off, verified" next to destination deals like "Boston stays from $135/night" — none of which are codes at all. Another promotes a "$50 off sitewide, no promo code needed" deal alongside an FAQ about free shipping, which is a strange thing for a vacation rental platform to offer. A third site's own data is more honest than its headline: of 70 Vrbo offers it tracked over 24 months, about 65% were ordinary listing-level deals and 35% were "up to X" capped promotions — the kind of headline number almost nobody hits.

The FTC has repeatedly warned consumers about misleading coupon claims online, and Vrbo is a prime example of how the genre works: take real, narrow discounts, slap a code-shaped headline on them, and collect ad or affiliate clicks from people hunting for savings that were never gated behind a code in the first place.

Hosts are the exception. Hosts occasionally receive coupons toward an annual subscription when setting up their first listing, redeemable during signup — but that's a host-side cost, not something a guest booking a cabin for the weekend can touch.
